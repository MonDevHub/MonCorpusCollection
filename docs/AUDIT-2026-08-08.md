## Critical

### C1 — MIT licence applied over CC BY-SA and unlicensed news text

**Location:** `LICENSE:1`, `README.md:128`, `README.md:131-140` (was `README.md:82`,
`README.md:87-88`)

**Evidence.** `LICENSE` is a verbatim MIT grant, `Copyright (c) 2025 MonDevHub`. The README's own
Sources table states:

| source | shard prefix | licence as stated in the README |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mon Wikipedia | `wikipedia_shard_*` | **CC BY-SA** |
| Mon News Agency (IMNA) | `monnews_shard_*` | *(none stated)* |
| MonDictDB | `mondictdb_shard_*` | MIT |

Measured mass: Wikipedia is 25,589,404 of 47,221,746 counted characters (**54.2%**); monnews a
further 12,064,310 (**25.5%**). *(Re-measured 2026-08-12 after the PII redaction; was
25,590,114 of 47,225,104 and 12,066,365 at 25.6%.)*

**Reasoning.** CC BY-SA is copyleft with ShareAlike. Redistributing it under MIT strips both the
ShareAlike obligation and the attribution the licence requires. The IMNA rows are worse: 12M
characters of a working news agency's archive, redistributed under a permissive grant this project
has no authority to issue.

**Impact.** Every downstream consumer inherits a defective grant, including MonOCR, which
`README.md:5` names as a dependent. This is the finding that blocks institutional adoption.

**Fix.** Relicense the corpus to CC BY-SA 4.0 with a per-source NOTICE, and either obtain IMNA
permission or drop those shards. This is an owner decision, not an engineering one.

**Confidence:** high. Both halves are quoted from the repo's own files.

### C2 — Personal contact data redistributed in the shards

**Location:** `shards/facebook_shard_001.txt`, `shards/custom_shard_001.txt`,
`shards/wikipedia_shard_001.txt`, and five others

**Evidence.** Pattern scan across `shards/*.txt`, counts only — the matched values are deliberately
not reproduced here:

```
email-shaped matches : 4
phone-shaped matches : 106
shards affected      : 8 of 15
```

`shards/facebook_shard_001.txt` contains a numbered roster pairing personal names with mobile
numbers.

> **Re-measured 2026-08-12.** The 106 figure came from a loose "long digit run" pattern and was
> mostly ISBNs, DOIs, years and populations. Against a pattern anchored on the Myanmar and Thai
> dialling plans, and covering Myanmar numerals as well as ASCII, the true figures were:
>
> ```
> email-shaped matches :  4   (3 in monnews_shard_002, 1 in wikipedia_shard_001)
> phone-shaped matches : 23   (20 ASCII numerals, 3 Myanmar numerals)
> shards affected      :  6 of 15
> ```
>
> The 3 Myanmar-numeral numbers, all in `monnews_shard_001`, are the ones worth noting: U+1040–
> U+1049 are not `\d`, so an ASCII-only scan reports them as absent. Twelve remaining long digit
> runs were inspected individually and are ISBN-10s, one DOI, one value in AU and one Zoom
> meeting ID — none personal.

**Reasoning.** Neither `README.md` nor `docs/CORPUS.md` mentions personal data at any point.
`docs/CORPUS.md:31-33` documents the cleaning that is performed — zero-width characters, control
codes, URLs — and PII is not in that list, so its absence is not an oversight in one shard but an
absent policy. *(Was cited as `docs/CORPUS.md:22-24`.)*

**Impact.** Personal data republished under a permissive grant with no takedown path. A language
model pretrained on this corpus memorises it, and MonCorpusCollection is explicitly intended as
pretraining data.

**Fix.** A redaction pass before the next release. The roster line needs removing outright rather
than pattern-masking.

**Confidence:** high. Reproduced by direct scan.

> **Half-closed 2026-08-12.** The working tree is clean: 21 lines removed across six shards,
> whole lines rather than masks, and `scripts/data_quality.py --check` now fails the build if an
> email or mobile shape reappears. `docs/CORPUS.md:64` records the policy.
>
> **The finding stays open** because the git history still contains every removed line. Until the
> `git filter-repo` rewrite in `docs/NEXT_STEPS.md` is run and force-pushed, a clone reaches the
> pre-redaction contents with one command. The Hugging Face publication gate stays in force.

## High

### H1 — `docs/CORPUS.md` says URLs are stripped; 9,228 remain

`docs/CORPUS.md:33` (was `:24`) states the importer *"Removes control codes (`\x00-\x1F`) and URLs"*, and
`scripts/build_shards.py:89` `URL_RE` (was `:49`) does implement it. But the rule was applied inconsistently:
`monnews_shard_001.txt` carries 3,715 URLs and `wikipedia_shard_003.txt` 5,457, while
`wikipedia_shard_001/002/004` have none and instead carry 1,975 / 1,670 / 545 orphan `"` lines — the
residue of a stripped "Retrieved from" prefix.

The importer is dated 2026-07-26; the affected shards are dated 2026-05-21. The spec documents the
current importer and was presented as describing the corpus. It does not.

### H2 — The deduplication claim understates the real figure by an order of magnitude

`README.md:39` said *"A small number of short segments still recur across shards."* **That sentence
no longer exists** — it was replaced by the measured paragraph now at `README.md:65-70`, which is
the fix this finding asked for. Measured with the repo's own dedup key (`build_shards.py:142-147` `skeleton_key`, was `:76-85`,
applied per cleaned non-blank line):

- 369,898 eligible lines to 275,892 unique — **94,006 redundant instances (25.4%)**, 4,708,496
  redundant characters (**10.0% of the corpus**)
- 4,542 distinct skeletons appear in more than one shard
- duplicated lines are not short: median 63 characters, p90 180, max 2,575

> **Corrected 2026-08-12.** The original figures — 277,730 unique, 92,183 redundant, 24.9%,
> 4,582,660 characters — were internally inconsistent: 369,913 − 277,730 is 92,183, but the unique
> count itself was wrong. Re-measured over 15 shards, pre-redaction, the numbers were 275,906
> unique / 94,007 redundant / 25.4%; the figures above are the same measurement after the C2
> redaction removed 21 lines. Both are reproducible with `python3 scripts/data_quality.py`, which
> exists because every figure in this finding originally came from uncommitted throwaway code.
> The p90 is 180 rather than 178, a percentile-convention difference, not a data change.

**Impact.** `README.md:107` (was `:47`) frames shards as the distribution unit, so a user splitting
train/eval by file gets contamination across ~4,500 skeletons including 2.5 KB passages. That
silently inflates whatever they report.

### H3 — Root `results/` ships character counts from a different pipeline

`results/summary.json` records `"output_dir": "analysis_results"`, `"files_seen": 8823`, and
`"total_nga_to_mon_nga_replacements": 438900` — generated with `--normalize-mon-nga` over raw
scraper files that are not in this repo.

Consequence:

| file | `င` U+1004 | `ၚ` U+105A |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| `results/character_frequency.csv` | **row absent** | 1,143,707 |
| `results/latest/character_frequency.csv` | 579,086 | 900,749 |

The stale CSV carries no marker that 438,900 `င` were rewritten. A reader concludes Burmese Nga does
not occur in Mon text. `README.md:98` (was `:56`) documents only `results/latest/`, so these four
stale trees are undocumented and unowned.

## What is good, specifically

- **The headline statistics are exactly true.** Running `scripts/shard_stats.py` reproduces the
  README table cell for cell: 15 shards, 1,253,139 lines, 47,221,746 characters, 82.6% Mon/Myanmar.
  *(Was 1,253,172 and 47,225,104 pre-redaction. Still true, against the current numbers.)*
- **`results/latest/` is reproducible from the tracked shards.** Regenerated 2026-08-12 over all
  15; it previously described 14 and had no row for `handwritten_shard_001.txt`.

  > Corrected 2026-08-12: this claimed `total_raw_text_length: 52,156,464` "matches an independent
  > byte count exactly", and that was wrong twice. The value is a **character** count — `len()` over
  > the decoded text — and the byte count is 2.49x larger, because Myanmar code points take three
  > UTF-8 bytes each. It also covered 14 shards, not 15. Current values: 52,256,919 characters
  > against 130,428,126 bytes. The real cross-check is `total_counted_characters: 47,221,746`, which
  > matches `shard_stats.py`'s character total exactly — two independent scripts agreeing.
- **Encoding hygiene is better than the docs claim.** All 15 shards are 100% NFC with zero U+FFFD,
  zero control characters, zero CRLF, zero BOM/ZWSP/ZWNJ/ZWJ. Orphan combining marks are 0.002%,
  far inside the corpus's own 1.0% bar. For a scraped Myanmar-script corpus this is unusual.
- **`build_shards.py` is append-only by construction.** `flush_shard` refuses to overwrite
  (`:354-355` in `flush_shard`, was `:215-216`), and the dedup key is deliberately biased toward keeping. Refusing to destroy prior
  data is the right default for a corpus.
- **Honest self-disclosure.** `README.md:150-155` (was `:104-109`) volunteers that the OCR shard
  carries recognition errors and that MonDictDB's MT-exclusion is a property of the importer rather
  than the file. `README.md:95` (was `:53`) flags the Gemini shard as LLM-authored and unverified,
  and it is separable by filename. Most corpus repos hide this.

## Roadmap

**Immediate.** C1 and C2. Both carry legal weight and neither is an engineering task.

**Next.** H2 and H3 — these are truthfulness problems in a repo whose product *is* its
truthfulness. Then H1, and commit the handwritten shard or remove it from the README table.

**Long-term.** A checksum manifest, and a data-validation script asserting the invariants this audit
had to measure by hand: NFC, no URLs, no PII patterns, cross-shard dedup.

> Closed 2026-08-12. `shards/SHA256SUMS` covers all 15 shards, and `scripts/data_quality.py`
> computes every figure in this document that was previously measured by uncommitted throwaway
> code — blank-line ratio and per-shard share, dedup rate with its denominator, URL count,
> PII-pattern count in both numeral systems, and newline termination. `--check` turns the PII
> figure into a build gate. The NFC assertion is not in it: `corpus_counter_normalized.py`
> normalizes on read, so nothing currently re-measures NFC conformance of the files on disk.

## Deliberately not recommended

- **A `pyproject.toml` or `requirements.txt`.** All four scripts are stdlib-only, so the manifest
  would be empty. Adding packaging here is ceremony.
- **A test suite.** For a data repo the meaningful check is data validation, not unit tests.
  `scripts/data_quality.py` is that check; it asserts against the corpus rather than against
  mocked inputs, which is the form that catches what actually goes wrong here.
